Research prototype from a bachelor's thesis

An app for couples in long-distance relationships.

Unapart connects exactly two people in a shared, end-to-end encrypted space: chat, status, a shared journal, milestones and games for two. Built on the open Matrix protocol.

Currently by invitation only. Unapart is being tested in a study via Apple's TestFlight and is not available on the App Store.

How Unapart handles data Support

What Unapart is

  • Chat for two

    Messages and stickers, end-to-end encrypted. The server only stores them encrypted.

  • Status, not location

    Show that you're around or busy, or share nothing at all. A status only arrives if you have both agreed to the Partner View.

  • Pause

    Pause sharing for an hour, the rest of the day, three days or a time you choose. Your partner then sees "No status shared" – exactly as with "Hidden".

  • Journal and milestones

    A shared journal with photos, your milestones and a shared garden.

  • Play together

    Connect Four, Memory, Word Chain, Prompt Deck and more games for two.

  • You decide

    "What am I sharing?" shows your shares. Notification windows set when your iPhone shows notifications. If you like, Face ID or your passcode locks the app.

Who it is for

For couples who live in different places and take part in the study for the bachelor's thesis. Each partner needs their own iPhone. Unapart is built for exactly two people. Accounts are issued by the study lead; there is no open sign-up.

What Unapart deliberately does not do

  • No location sharing. Unapart does not ask for your location.
  • No read receipts, no typing indicator, no automatic online indicator.
  • No one-sided visibility. A status only arrives if you have both agreed.
  • No notification when you take back a share or take a pause. To be honest: your partner may still notice it from a changed display.
  • No labels. Unapart neither shows nor stores an attachment style.
  • No streaks to break and no leaderboard.
  • No ads, no tracking, no usage statistics sent to the study lead.
  • No crash reports without your consent. The switch is off by default.

What the server can and cannot see is described in the privacy policy.

Background

Unapart is the prototype of a bachelor's thesis in media informatics at Hochschule Düsseldorf (University of Applied Sciences Düsseldorf). The study asks which privacy features couples use and how they experience them – not what they write to each other.

Unapart is free software under the GNU AGPL-3.0. The source code is available on request, see Support.
